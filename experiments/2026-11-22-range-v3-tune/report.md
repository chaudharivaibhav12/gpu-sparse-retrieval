# Range scorer v3-tune: unrolled decode and radix merge (report)

Experiment `2026-11-22-range-v3-tune`, branch `exp/arnav/2026-11-22-range-v3-tune`. Protocol and amendments: [configs/protocol.json](configs/protocol.json). Calibration queries only (500; profiles use the first 25); held-out queries untouched. Only CUDA 12.6 builds on cuda5 count; laptop numbers are labelled development readings.

## 1. Summary

- **Final variant: u4m** = change A (unrolled decode, U=4) + change D (last-block merge by compaction + radix select), at **W=4096**, 256 threads, radix crossover 1. It is now the default build of `src/cuda_range_v2.cu` on this branch.
- Measurement E (cuda5, 500 calibration queries, paired, crossed bootstrap): u4m at W=4096 / v3-3 at W=4096 = **0.836 [0.831, 0.840]** at k=10, **0.617 [0.611, 0.622]** at k=100, **0.245 [0.241, 0.248]** at k=1000; against the team reference ctrl (V2-5 at W=1024): **0.911, 0.308, 0.128**.
- Full collection (Measurement F, section 10): u4m is 0.577 / 0.172 / 0.069 x ctrl (V2-5) at k = 10 / 100 / 1000, and 1.55 / 0.66 / 0.22 x the team's pruned scorer prune-tm (calibration only, after the freeze).
- Change B (zero-score skipping / aggregated bucket adds) was negative on laptop development readings and dropped before timing; change C (result buffer) was dropped on the laptop profile.
- Decision rule E1 was applied per W at W=4096 by the owner after the run (protocol amendment); under the clause registered first, the final variant would have been v3-3 (section 3).

**Amendment (owner, 2026-11-22).** E1 is applied per W at W=4096, because W=4096 with 256 threads was chosen as the configuration before Measurement E (this experiment's Step 1 prompt and the owner's 2026-11-21 decision) and E1 compares each variant with v3-3 at the same W. The clause "must pass at every W it was timed at" had been added by Claude when registering the rule. The run's own analysis files are left as produced under the registered clause.

**Not adopted, recorded.** v3-3 at W=1024 is the fastest k=10 cell (0.0907 ms against u4m at W=4096 0.0987 ms; ratio to ctrl 0.827 vs 0.911). Using it for k=10 would need a second index (W=1024) and a second code path (the v3-3 merge), so it is not adopted. D loses at W=1024, k=10 (1.134 x v3-3; u4m 1.065): with 977 ranges and k=10 the merge's fixed costs (per-range survivor search, prefix sum over 977 ranges, compaction, a radix select over a few thousand keys, several barriers) exceed the small warp-shuffle merge it replaces.

## 6. Change B: negative result (laptop development readings)

Dropped from timing by the owner on these laptop readings (laptop GPU, CUDA 13.3, 1M, W=4096, crossover 1, 25 queries x 10 rounds, one process per variant; development readings, not results). GPU span relative to v3-3:

| variant | k=10 | k=100 | k=1000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| skip0 (B2) | 1.02 | 0.99 | 1.06 |
| aggskip (B1 + B2) | 1.08 | 1.02 | 1.05 |

The mechanism is real: the exact CPU replay of the radix passes (25 queries x 245 ranges) found about 48% of a range's documents score 0, the first pass's largest bucket (digit 0) holds more than half the keys in about 97% of ranges, and a warp's bucket atomicAdd in pass 1 is about 30 of 32 lanes deep on one address. But the radix select + gather was only about 8% of the pre-merge critical path (laptop profile), and the extra nonzero count and `__match_any_sync` cost about as much as the skipped work. The code stays behind its switch, off.

## 7. Failures and process issues

- **Measurement P (profile of v3-3) never ran**: the GPU was never idle in its 4 h wait (other users' jobs at every one of 481 checks); stopped 08:22Z, not re-queued (owner). Replaced by Measurement P2.
- **Measurement E session 1** waited for P, then never saw an idle GPU before its 6 h deadline (59 checks); stopped at 13:20Z with no GPU process. Session 2 (relaunch) got an idle GPU at 17:40Z but:
- **False overlaps (monitoring bug).** The overlap check classified the fixture step's own short-lived scorer subprocesses (which exit before their owner can be read) as other users' processes, so every fixture attempt was flagged. Session 2 was stopped by hand; the classifier now identifies our own short-lived processes by executable (amendment; commit 7968051).
- **Gate rule changed to contended** (owner): gates are correctness checks; session 3 was stopped by hand after gate ctrl-03 passed; session 4 ran the six v3 gates contended (u2m and u4m gates overlapped other users' jobs in 11 and 14 steps; correctness unaffected) and the timing under the full idle rule.
- **Disk full on the laptop's /tmp** (a 3.9 GB RAM-backed volume) from fixture outputs kept with `--output`; one fixture run died; scratch outputs were deleted (owner's OK) and the run repeated; later laptop dry runs were archived to run-bulk with a SHA-256 manifest.
- **`pkill -f` matched its own ssh command** when stopping session 2 (a repeat of an earlier lesson); the remaining processes were stopped by exact PID.
- **Resource-table script** crashed on ctrl's untemplated kernel during session 1 (non-fatal; fixed).
- **Process time**: at k=1000 each timing process spends most of its time in the oracle check (about 11.5 M result rows in Python), so the session took about 2 h for about 25 min of GPU time.
- Every stopped attempt is committed with a STATUS file; large originals are in run-bulk with SHA-256 and cuda5 custody.

## 9. Diagnostic interpretation

- **D removed the merge bottleneck.** In v3-3 the last block's warp-shuffle merge (`merge_lists`) grows with k: merge tail 8, 66 and 641 µs at k = 10, 100, 1000 (clock build), about 80% of the kernel at k=1000, and most of it is warps waiting at the merge's one barrier (S7: 560 µs at k=1000) while warp 0's selection rounds run. D replaces it with a block-wide pipeline whose cost scales with the number of survivors, not with k rounds: merge tail 8, 13 and 68 µs. At k=1000 the remaining merge time is mostly compaction (28.5 µs: reading every surviving 24-byte candidate from global memory) and the radix select over the survivors (14.8 µs). D gives nothing at k=10 at W=4096 (the old merge was already only 8 µs) and loses at W=1024, k=10, where its fixed costs over 977 ranges exceed the small merge it replaces.
- **A hid load latency in decode.** decode+add on the critical path falls from about 79-82 µs to 55-56 µs at every k (mean per block 14 -> 10-11 µs). In v3-3 the encoded-word load was immediately consumed and the line holding it carried 49% of decode samples, 86% of them long-scoreboard stalls; with four postings' loads issued before any use, the load lines carry about 1% of samples and the remaining wait (17% of decode samples) sits at the first use of the loaded words. Coalescing did not change (2.61 sectors per encoded request against an ideal of 2.58; impacts 2.13 against 1.00) and DRAM throughput stays low (8-11% of peak), so decode remains latency-bound rather than bandwidth-bound. u4m always loads both encoded words, doubling encoded load instructions (2.58 M vs 1.29 M warp requests), which costs nothing measurable.
- **The next bottleneck in decode is the term lookup.** In u4m, 56% of decode samples are the per-posting binary search over `count_start` (which term owns work-list item g), with no memory stalls: pure instruction and shared-memory work, repeated for every posting. A per-warp or per-tile term cursor (the term changes rarely between consecutive items) would remove most of it; this is the clearest target for a next change.
- **What is left on the critical path (u4m, W=4096).** Pre-merge critical path about 69-81 µs: decode+add 55-56 µs, radix select+gather 7-10 µs, bitonic sort 1-8 µs (grows with k), setup about 4 µs plus the S1 barrier about 3 µs. With 245 ranges and 4 blocks per SM (shared memory limit; 3 at k=1000), the launch is 1.33 waves (1.78 at k=1000): the slowest ranges of the second wave set the kernel time, so the densest ranges matter more than the average.
- **Radix contention (change B) is real but small.** The replay shows nearly fully serialized bucket atomics in pass 1, yet the radix select+gather is about 7-10 µs on the critical path; B's extra work cost about as much as it saved (section 6). The radix stages' sample share rises in u4m (27.9% -> 31.4% at k=10) only because decode shrank.
- **Host side.** Per query outside the kernel: launch API about 13 µs, launch to kernel start about 12 µs, D2H copy 1-2.4 µs, kernel end to copy return 12-22 µs; unprofiled wall minus GPU busy is 21-34 µs. At k=10 this host share (about 20% of the 106 µs u4m wall) is now comparable to the time the decode change saved; change C (pinned/zero-copy results) would address only the copy part (a few µs).
- **Resources.** u4m uses 48 registers (v3-3 40) and 1.3 KB more static shared memory; achieved occupancy is unchanged or higher (51 -> 53% at k=10, 39 -> 44% at k=1000) because shared memory, not registers, limits residency at W=4096.
